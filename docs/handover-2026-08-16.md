# Handover — 2026-08-16

A day spent making local models viable, then building two things on top of that:
an instrument for measuring whether a model still plays a character, and signals
intelligence. Everything below is merged to `main` and pushed.

## What changed, and why

### 1. Local models became usable

`reasoning_effort = "none"` was the whole fix. `qwen3.5-4b` had been spending
5,206 completion tokens per call *thinking* — 13× a working baseline — because
the `[llm.params.thinking]` table in `config.toml` is Moonshot's spelling and
LM Studio silently drops unknown fields. It looked configured and did nothing.

Turn time went from ~205 s to ~32 s. `qwen/qwen3.5-9b` then measured **~30 s per
turn at 97% first-try order validity** — faster than Kimi over the network, free,
and level with it on validity. A bigger local model was also the faster one,
because it retries less and writes tighter.

`config.example.toml` and the README now carry the per-backend spellings and the
measured numbers. Note the trap recorded there: `/no_think` in a prompt measured
**30× worse** than doing nothing, because a build without that control token
reasons about why you wrote it.

### 2. Four fixes that fell out of playing

- **Movement rejections now name the legal destinations.** `engine/orders.py`
  already knew this lesson — the comment above the out-of-reach check explains
  it — but the branch two lines up returned a bare "needs an objective". That is
  the dominant failure shape once reasoning is off. Orders defused to `defend`
  dropped from 4 to 1 per four turns.
- **Model warm-up before the fan-out.** A JIT-loading LM Studio model answers
  with tokens billed but *empty* content, so 8 of 9 commanders burned a repair
  on turn 1 of every game. `LMStudioClient.warm_up()`, awaited in
  `gather_orders`. Verified against a genuinely evicted model: 8 empties → 0.
- **`play_campaign.py` no longer crashes** on the `staff` dispatch (it indexed
  `campaign.dossiers` with a pseudo-id).
- **The prompt now names who reads the dispatch.** See the CLAUDE.md principle
  about unfilled slots. Head-of-state salutations went 6/72 → 0/36.

### 3. Staff-option divergence — `analyze_divergence.py`

Scores each order against the staff options that corps was offered:
`first` / `middle` / `hold` / `off-menu` / `unscored`. Free, offline.

| | first | middle | hold | off-menu |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| qwen3.5-4b | 30% | 9% | **51%** | 9% |
| qwen/qwen3.5-9b | 41–47% | 9–12% | 21–23% | 23% |

**4b's collapse mode is inaction, not obedience.** The metric as originally
scoped — "fraction of orders not matching staff option #1" — would have scored
that as *independence*. That is why the buckets exist.

It took two rounds of review to get right, and both found the same class of bug:
a model failure scored as the strongest evidence of character. Read the
"never let a metric's good news absorb its bad news" principle in CLAUDE.md
before changing what `off-menu` counts.

### 4. Signals intelligence

Occasionally decrypts one enemy commander's **validated** orders from last week
(WEGO makes this-week decrypts impossible; turn 1 can never intercept). Spec:
`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-02-signals-intelligence-design.md`.

**Delivery is deliberately asymmetric, and this is the thing most likely to be
"fixed" by mistake:**

- The **player's** decrypt goes to his **inbox only**. His commanders learn
  nothing until he signals one.
- The **AI side** broadcasts into every briefing on that side, because there is
  no player there to distribute it.

`test_an_axis_intercept_reaches_the_inbox_and_no_axis_briefing` guards it by
asserting both halves. The inbox card must keep its closing line — *"This
decrypt has not been circulated"* — or the player watches his commanders ignore
a decrypt he never sent and blames the commanders.

## What we learned playing it, and the decision taken

Measured over 18 turns of live play:

| | commander-turns with a decrypt | referenced it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| qwen3.5-9b | 24 | 0 (0%) |
| kimi-k2.6 | 20 | 3 (15%) |

Kimi reasons about it well — *"Guderian's panzers mass at Borisov… this is the
classic German stroke: a pincer on Smolensk"* — but **not one Soviet order across
either run went off-menu onto a region only the decrypt named**. Where a decrypt
looked decisive, the staff had already proposed that move as option #1. The
decrypt supplies rationale, not decisions, because `_staff_options` is what the
model acts on and it knows nothing about the intel.

**Decision (2026-08-16): keep it as flavour.** It works as designed on the
player's side, it reads well on a strong model, and the Soviets may respond to
it occasionally. Revisit later if wanted.

If it is ever revisited, the lever is letting `_staff_options` propose a counter
when intel names a region in range. That is defensible — the staff already
compute from full state, so it changes what the *staff* know, not what the
commander knows — but it is a real design change and should be brainstormed, not
slipped in.

## Open items

Nothing is blocked. In rough priority order:

1. **`build_briefing`'s side fallback.** `side = own[0].side if own else "axis"`
   silently briefs a corps-less commander as axis. Unreachable in the shipped
   game (every caller filters to `active_commanders` first) but an unguarded dev
   script hit it within 12 turns. Raising would be better than defaulting.
2. **`Corps.from_dict` does not clamp `strength` to `max_strength`.** Only bites
   a hand-edited save; the game cannot produce the state itself. Recommendation
   is to raise rather than clamp, or leave it — a silent clamp would rewrite a
   save the player edited on purpose.
3. **Frequency of intercepts** is set at `Campaign.intel_chance = 0.25`. Four
   decrypts in eleven eligible turns felt right in play; adjust after a longer
   campaign if it feels wrong.
4. **The divergence baseline rests on 4-turn runs**, so per-commander rows sit on
   6–20 orders. The `ALL` rows are solid; individual commanders are indicative.

## Small things worth knowing

- The scratch playtest runners live in the session scratchpad, not the repo. The
  pattern is in CLAUDE.md: build the `Campaign` yourself, save to a scratch path,
  hash `server/saves/campaign.json` before and after.
- Set `PYTHONIOENCODING=utf-8` when piping a playtest to a file on Windows, or a
  commander writing "Białystok" will kill the run with a cp1252 error.
- `config - *.toml` is now gitignored. It was not, and `config - kimi.toml`
  holds a live API key — it was one `git add -A` from being published.
