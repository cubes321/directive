# Handover — 2026-09-30

Last night's playtests on qwen3.5-9b turned up a lot of "LLM problems". This
session found that almost none of them were failures of the LLM calls: all 134
order calls in those runs came back valid first time. The problems were in how
the prompts were put together, plus one Kimi quirk and two engine rules. Twelve
commits, all on `main`, **not pushed** (`main` is 12 ahead of `origin/main`).

### Engine

- **`1abe47e` Move-in order.** When more corps reach a captured region than it
  can hold, faster formations now go in first (`BASE_MP`, then id). Before this,
  `ix_ak`/`vii_ak` sorted ahead of `xlvi_pz`, so infantry filled Minsk and
  Smolensk and the panzers were left outside.
- **`3a40ab4` Garrison hold-back** (the user's rule, both sides). When every
  corps in an urban region or live OKH objective is ordered out, none is ordered
  in, and an enemy-held region borders it, the slowest corps stays. Replaying
  the recorded games, it saved each city in the week it was lost: Smolensk in
  game A, Minsk in B, and Minsk in P2 (standing 5 → 13). The user's alternative,
  a weak phantom "security unit", was tested first and saved none. The
  countermand reaches the commander's war record and makes a pop-up likelier.
  Live, Hoth protested unprompted, in character, the week it happened.

## Open items, roughly by value

1. **Dispatches don't match what the corps did.** "I hold Baranovichi as
   directed" after leaving it; Weichs "holds Minsk with both corps" after moving
   one away. Idea from last night: the staff report compares each dispatch with
   the actual moves ("Guderian reports Lida; his corps are at Slonim"). That
   turns a defect into a feature: the player catches boasting generals.
2. **The staff report misreads deadlines on 9b.** It twice said Smolensk had
   missed its week-9 deadline in weeks 5 and 7. A cheap fix to try: put "N weeks
   left" in the objective fact instead of "deadline week 9".
3. **OKH scoring punishes early success.** Taking an objective and losing it
   before the deadline costs more than never taking it (−6 vs −3). The garrison
   rule makes that rarer but doesn't change the rule. This is a design question
   for the user.
4. **Garrison rule, Soviet side:** balance is unmeasured. In replays it held
   Soviet armies in Minsk, Mogilev and Vitebsk. Watch whether it slows the
   German advance in a long game.
5. **The garrison keeps the slowest corps,** so a lone panzer corps gets held,
   as Hoth's LVII was.
6. **Pop-ups can paraphrase the player's own directive** (Guderian urged the
   breakout I had just ordered). The echo guard catches copies, not paraphrases.
7. The staff prompt is German-side only; a Soviet player would get von Bock's
   staff.
8. Residual 9b quirks: occasional "Monsieur", "My Führer, Generaloberst von
   Bock", and invented corps ("XVIII Corps").

## How to measure things cheaply (all free)

- **Resend recorded prompts.** Every order transcript in `logs/run-*/campaign/`
  holds the exact request. Swap one sentence and resend to LM Studio. That is
  how the Berlin wording was settled (60 decision points × variants, ~5 min).
- **Replay recorded orders** (`commanders/replay.py`) for engine rules. A rule
  that moves corps makes later recorded orders stop fitting, so compare against
  the *old* behaviour for everything else, and trust only the week of first
  divergence. Replaying today's games under the new move-in order drifted to
  42 broken orders.
- **Sandbox games through the real web API:** scratchpad wrapper around
  `server.app` with `_session.save_path` set to a scratch file, on port 8010 via
  a temporary `launch.json` entry. About 33 s/turn on 9b.
- The probe scripts from this session live in the session scratchpad and will
  be wiped. Rebuild them from the descriptions in commit messages.
